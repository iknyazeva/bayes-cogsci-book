# Binary Logistic Regression

## 1. The Generative Model for Binary Outcomes

When the outcome variable is binary ($y_i \in \{0, 1\}$, such as decision choice, task success, medical diagnosis, or memory recall), standard linear regression models fail because they violate foundational probability axioms.

Instead, Bayesian logistic regression defines a proper **Bernoulli likelihood** coupled to a **logistic sigmoid link**:

$$
y_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i), \quad p_i = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\alpha + \beta x_i) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\alpha + \beta x_i)}}
$$

```
                   Forward Generative Workflow:
                   
  Predictor (x) ──► [ Linear: η = α + βx ] ──► [ Sigmoid: p = logit⁻¹(η) ] ──► [ Bernoulli: y ∈ {0, 1} ]
```

### Why Standard Linear Regression Fails for Binary Outcomes:
- **Invalid Probability Predictions**: An unconstrained linear model $\hat{y}_i = \alpha + \beta x_i$ predicts impossible numbers outside the unit interval (e.g., probabilities of $-0.25$ or $+1.30$).
- **Heteroskedastic Error Coupling**: For a binary Bernoulli trial, the variance is inherently linked to the mean: $\operatorname{Var}(y_i \mid p_i) = p_i (1 - p_i)$. Variance peaks at $p = 0.5$ ($\operatorname{Var} = 0.25$) and collapses to zero as $p \to 0$ or $p \to 1$.
- **Non-Normal Errors**: The residuals $y_i - p_i$ can take only two discrete values ($1 - p_i$ or $-p_i$), violating Gaussian normality assumptions.

## 2. Mathematical Architecture: Log-Odds and the Sigmoid Link

To ensure that the predicted probability $p_i$ is strictly bounded in the open interval $(0, 1)$, logistic regression operates on the **log-odds** (logit) scale:

### 2.1 The Odds and Logit Transformation
The **odds of success** is the ratio of the probability of success to the probability of failure:

$$
\text{Odds}(p_i) = \frac{p_i}{1 - p_i} \in (0, \infty)
$$

Taking the natural logarithm yields the **logit function**, which maps $(0, 1) \to (-\infty, \infty)$:

$$
\eta_i = \operatorname{logit}(p_i) = \log\left(\frac{p_i}{1 - p_i}\right) = \alpha + \beta_1 x_{i1} + \dots + \beta_k x_{ik}
$$

### 2.2 Inverting to the Logistic Sigmoid Function
To recover the probability $p_i$ from the linear predictor $\eta_i$, we invert the logit transformation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{p_i}{1 - p_i} &= e^{\eta_i} \\
p_i &= e^{\eta_i}(1 - p_i) \\
p_i (1 + e^{\eta_i}) &= e^{\eta_i} \\
p_i &= \frac{e^{\eta_i}}{1 + e^{\eta_i}} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\eta_i}} = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\eta_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2.3 The Bernoulli Likelihood Function
Given observations $\mathbf{y} = (y_1, \dots, y_N)^T$, the joint likelihood is the product of independent Bernoulli probabilities:

$$
p(\mathbf{y} \mid \alpha, \boldsymbol{\beta}) = \prod_{i=1}^N p_i^{y_i} (1 - p_i)^{1 - y_i} = \prod_{i=1}^N \left(\frac{1}{1 + e^{-\eta_i}}\right)^{y_i} \left(\frac{e^{-\eta_i}}{1 + e^{-\eta_i}}\right)^{1 - y_i}
$$

The log-likelihood is:

$$
\log \mathcal{L}(\alpha, \boldsymbol{\beta}) = \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ y_i \log(p_i) + (1 - y_i) \log(1 - p_i) \right] = \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ y_i \eta_i - \log(1 + e^{\eta_i}) \right]
$$

## 3. The Three Levels of Coefficient Interpretation

Because the logit link is non-linear, a regression slope $\beta$ has three distinct interpretations across different mathematical scales:

### 1. Log-Odds Scale ($\beta$)
- An additive 1-unit increase in $x$ produces an additive change of $\beta$ in the **log-odds of success**.
- *Example*: If $\beta = 0.8$, each additional unit of $x$ adds $0.8$ to $\log(\text{Odds})$.

### 2. Odds Ratio Scale ($\text{OR} = e^\beta$)
- An additive 1-unit increase in $x$ multiplies the **odds of success** by $e^\beta$:

$$
\frac{\text{Odds}(x + 1)}{\text{Odds}(x)} = \frac{e^{\alpha + \beta(x+1)}}{e^{\alpha + \beta x}} = e^\beta
$$

- *Example*: If $\beta = 0.8$, $\text{OR} = e^{0.8} \approx 2.23$. The odds of success are **$2.23\times$ higher** ($+123\%$ higher) per unit increase in $x$.

### 3. Probability Scale ($\Delta P$ & The Divide-by-4 Rule)
Because the logistic curve is an S-shape, the change in probability $\Delta P$ per unit change in $x$ is **not constant**—it depends on the baseline probability!
The derivative of the logistic function with respect to $x$ is:

$$
\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial x_i} = \beta \cdot p_i (1 - p_i)
$$

The derivative achieves its absolute maximum when $p_i = 0.5$ (where $p(1-p) = 0.25 = 1/4$). This establishes **Gelman's Divide-by-4 Rule**:

$$
\max \left( \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta x} \right) \approx \frac{\beta}{4}
$$

- *Example*: If $\beta = 0.8$, a 1-unit change in $x$ produces a probability increase of **at most $\frac{0.8}{4} = 0.20$** ($20$ percentage points).

- **Outcome ($y_i \in \{0, 1\}$)**: Whether the household switched to a neighboring safe well ($1 = \text{Switched}, 0 = \text{Did not switch}$).
- **Predictors**:
  - $\text{dist100}_i$: Distance to the nearest known safe well (in units of 100 meters, mean $\approx 0.48$).
  - $\text{arsenic}_i$: Arsenic concentration level in the current well (in $\mu\text{g/L}$, mean $\approx 1.66$).

## 5. Complete Separation and Bayesian Regularization

A notorious failure mode of classical maximum likelihood logistic regression is **complete separation**: when a covariate $x$ perfectly separates all 0s from 1s (e.g. all $x > 2.0$ have $y = 1$, and all $x \le 2.0$ have $y = 0$).

- **Classical MLE (`glm`)**: The log-likelihood is monotonically increasing as $\beta \to \infty$. The optimization algorithm diverges, reporting $\hat{\beta} = \infty$ with standard error $\text{SE} = \infty$.
- **Bayesian Solution**: Placing weakly informative priors (e.g. $\beta \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 2.0)$ or $\operatorname{Student-}t(4, 0, 2.5)$) acts as a principled regularizer, pulling infinite maximum-likelihood estimates back to realistic, finite credible intervals.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Binary outcomes require a **Bernoulli likelihood** with an **inverse logit link** $p_i = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\alpha + \boldsymbol{\beta}^T \mathbf{x}_i)$.
- Always interpret parameters on multiple scales: **Log-Odds ($\beta$)**, **Odds Ratios ($\exp(\beta)$)**, and **Probability Contrasts ($\Delta P \approx \beta/4$)**.
- Weakly informative priors prevent numerical divergence and infinite parameter estimates under **complete separation**.
