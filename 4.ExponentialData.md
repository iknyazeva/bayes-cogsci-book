# Bayesian Inference with Exponential Data

## Scenario
The **exponential distribution** models the time between events — how long we wait until something happens. In cognitive science, this arises naturally when studying **fixation durations** during reading or visual search, **inter-response times** in vigilance tasks, or **inter-spike intervals** in neural recordings. In each case, we observe a waiting time $Y$ that follows an exponential distribution with an unknown rate parameter $\lambda$:

$$
Y \sim \text{Exponential}(\lambda), \quad E[Y] = \frac{1}{\lambda}
$$

A higher rate $\lambda$ means shorter waiting times on average (more frequent events).

## Gamma Distribution as a Conjugate Prior

The **gamma distribution** is the conjugate prior for an **exponential likelihood**. This means that if the likelihood follows an exponential distribution, then the posterior distribution will also follow a gamma distribution when we use a gamma prior.

### Setting the Prior
To define our prior, we need to think about the rate of the process. For example, if we are studying fixation durations during reading and expect an average fixation of about 250 ms ($\lambda = 4$ fixations/second), we set a Gamma prior on $\lambda$ such that:

$$
\frac{\text{shape}}{\text{rate}} = 4
$$

For instance, $\text{Gamma}(8, 2)$ gives us a prior mean of $8/2 = 4$ fixations/second and a standard deviation of $\sqrt{8}/2 \approx 1.4$, expressing moderate uncertainty.

A stronger prior like $\text{Gamma}(100, 25)$ has the same mean ($100/25 = 4$) but much less uncertainty (standard deviation $\sqrt{100}/25 = 0.4$), equivalent to having already observed 25 seconds of fixation data.

### Likelihood and Prior
The likelihood of observing a waiting time $Y$ given rate $\lambda$ is:

$$
L(\lambda \mid Y) = \lambda e^{-\lambda Y}
$$

The gamma prior for $\lambda$ is:

$$
\pi(\lambda) \propto \lambda^{\alpha - 1} e^{-\beta \lambda}
$$

Multiplying the likelihood and prior gives the posterior distribution:

$$
\pi(\lambda \mid Y) \propto \lambda^{\alpha + 1 - 1} e^{-(\beta + Y)\lambda}
$$

This is in the form of a **gamma distribution**. For multiple observations:

```{important}
**Gamma-Exponential posterior.** With a $\text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)$ prior on the rate $\lambda$ and $n$ exponential observations $Y_1, \ldots, Y_n$:

$$
\lambda \mid Y \sim \text{Gamma}\left(\alpha + n, \;\beta + \sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_i\right)
$$

The shape parameter increases by $n$ (number of events observed), and the rate parameter increases by the total waiting time. The prior parameter $\alpha$ acts as a pseudo-count of prior events, and $\beta$ as a pseudo-total of prior waiting time.
```

### Example: Fixation Durations During Reading

Suppose a researcher studies how quickly readers process words in a passage. She models individual fixation durations as exponential with rate $\lambda$ and sets a $\text{Gamma}(8, 2)$ prior (prior mean = 4 fixations/second, i.e., 250 ms per fixation).

After recording $n = 40$ fixations with a total duration of $\sum Y_i = 12$ seconds (average fixation = 300 ms), the posterior is:

$$
\lambda \mid Y \sim \text{Gamma}(8 + 40, \; 2 + 12) = \text{Gamma}(48, 14)
$$

The posterior mean is $48/14 \approx 3.43$ fixations/second (about 292 ms per fixation). The data suggests slightly longer fixations than expected — perhaps the passage contained difficult vocabulary.

```{admonition} Cognitive Science Example: Inter-Response Times in Sustained Attention
:class: tip
In a psychomotor vigilance task (PVT), a participant presses a button whenever they detect a stimulus. The **inter-response time** (IRT) between consecutive responses can be modeled as exponential with rate $\lambda$.

A researcher expects participants to respond approximately once every 5 seconds ($\lambda = 0.2$ responses/s) and sets a $\text{Gamma}(2, 10)$ prior (mean $0.2$, moderate uncertainty). After observing $n = 50$ responses with total IRT of $\sum Y_i = 280$ seconds, the posterior is:

$$
\lambda \mid Y \sim \text{Gamma}(2 + 50, \; 10 + 280) = \text{Gamma}(52, 290)
$$

The posterior mean $52/290 \approx 0.179$ suggests a slightly slower response rate than expected — possibly indicating fatigue effects during the task.
```

```{note}
**Classic textbook framing.** You may encounter the Gamma-Exponential model introduced using bus arrival times or machine failure intervals. The mathematics is identical — the exponential distribution models any process where events occur at a constant rate and the time between events is memoryless. The key cognitive science applications are fixation durations, inter-response times, inter-spike intervals, and time-to-event data in clinical studies.
```

## Key Takeaways
- The **Gamma distribution** is conjugate for an **exponential likelihood**: the posterior remains Gamma with updated shape and rate.
- The posterior updates simply: add the number of observations to the shape ($\alpha$), and the total observed time to the rate ($\beta$).
- The prior's **effective sample size** has two components: $\alpha$ pseudo-events over $\beta$ pseudo-time-units.
- With a strong prior (large $\alpha$ and $\beta$) and limited data, the posterior won't shift much.
- Exponential models are useful for **time-to-event** data in cognitive science: fixation durations, inter-response times, inter-spike intervals, and time to first saccade.
