# Part 6.3: Metropolis–Hastings

The Metropolis–Hastings algorithm shows how an unnormalised density can be converted into a valid Markov transition. It is especially valuable pedagogically because every proposed move and every rejection can be inspected.

## 1. The target need only be known up to proportionality

Let

$$
\widetilde p(\theta\mid y)=p(y\mid\theta)p(\theta)
$$

be proportional to the posterior. At the current state $\theta$, propose $\theta^*$ from $q(\theta^*\mid\theta)$ and calculate

$$
a(\theta,\theta^*)=
\min\left[
1,
\frac{\widetilde p(\theta^*\mid y)q(\theta\mid\theta^*)}
     {\widetilde p(\theta\mid y)q(\theta^*\mid\theta)}
\right].
$$

Draw $u\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)$. If $u<a$, move to $\theta^*$; otherwise repeat the current state. The posterior normalising constant cancels from the ratio.

The proposal ratio is essential for an asymmetric proposal. For a symmetric random walk,

$$
q(\theta^*\mid\theta)=q(\theta\mid\theta^*),
$$

so only the target-density ratio remains.

## 2. Why accept moves to lower-density regions?

Moves to a higher target density are accepted. Moves to a lower density can also be accepted, with smaller probability. This prevents the chain from climbing to a mode and remaining there. The stochastic rule produces the correct long-run frequency of visits rather than an optimisation trajectory.

The acceptance construction satisfies detailed balance under standard conditions. It therefore preserves the target distribution. Validity of the invariant distribution, however, does not guarantee efficient exploration.

## 3. Stable computation on the log scale

Products of likelihood terms can underflow numerically. Implement the decision with log densities:

$$
\log r=
\log\widetilde p(\theta^*\mid y)-\log\widetilde p(\theta\mid y)
+\log q(\theta\mid\theta^*)-\log q(\theta^*\mid\theta),
$$

and accept when

$$
\log u<\min(0,\log r).
$$

```python
def metropolis(log_target, initial, proposal_sd, draws, rng):
    chain = np.empty(draws)
    current = initial
    current_lp = log_target(current)

    for s in range(draws):
        proposed = rng.normal(current, proposal_sd)
        proposed_lp = log_target(proposed)
        log_accept = min(0.0, proposed_lp - current_lp)

        if np.log(rng.uniform()) < log_accept:
            current, current_lp = proposed, proposed_lp

        chain[s] = current       # repeated states must be retained

    return chain
```

This teaching implementation assumes a symmetric Normal proposal. Production code must include the proposal-density terms whenever that assumption is false.

## 4. Exact benchmark: a binomial survey proportion

Suppose 48 of 80 respondents support a proposal and

$$
\theta\sim\operatorname{Beta}(2,2),
\qquad
y\mid\theta\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(80,\theta).
$$

The exact posterior is $\operatorname{Beta}(50,34)$. This provides a benchmark for a random-walk Metropolis sampler on the logit scale,

$$
\eta=\operatorname{logit}(\theta),
\qquad
\eta^*\sim\mathcal N(\eta,\sigma_{\mathrm{prop}}^2).
$$

When constructing the target on the transformed scale, the Jacobian must be included unless the software handles the transformation automatically:

$$
\log p(\eta\mid y)
=\log p(\theta(\eta)\mid y)
+\log\{\theta(\eta)[1-\theta(\eta)]\}.
$$

Compare the simulated posterior mean, interval, and density with the exact Beta posterior. An attractive trace is not sufficient if the benchmark is missed beyond plausible MCSE.

## 5. Proposal scale is an efficiency problem

For a Normal random walk:

- a very small $\sigma_{\mathrm{prop}}$ yields many accepted but highly correlated moves;
- a very large $\sigma_{\mathrm{prop}}$ yields many rejections and repeated states;
- an intermediate scale may improve ESS per computation for the particular target.

## 6. Where random-walk Metropolis struggles

Performance deteriorates when:

- parameters are strongly correlated;
- the posterior has very different scales in different directions;
- separated modes are difficult to cross;
- boundaries or funnels create rapidly changing curvature;
- dimension increases without a well-adapted proposal.

A multivariate proposal can incorporate covariance, transformations can improve scale, and block updates can help. For differentiable continuous models, HMC and NUTS usually exploit geometry more effectively.

## 7. Warmup, rejected states, and multiple chains

- **Keep rejected states.** They are part of the Markov chain.
- **Separate warmup from retained draws.** Proposal tuning during warmup changes the transition rule; retained sampling should use the fixed adapted rule.
- **Run multiple chains.** One chain cannot reveal disagreement among starting regions.
- **Do not thin by default.** Thinning usually discards information and does not cure poor mixing.

## 9. Guided practice

````{dropdown} Question 1: Downhill moves in Metropolis-Hastings
A proposed point has lower posterior density than the current point. Is rejection certain?

**Answer.** No. The move is accepted with probability $\alpha = \widetilde{p}(\theta^* \mid y) / \widetilde{p}(\theta \mid y) < 1$. Occasional movement toward lower-density regions is essential for sampling the full distribution with correct frequency rather than merely locating its mode.
````

````{dropdown} Question 2: High acceptance with low ESS
A chain accepts 99% of proposals but has a very small ESS. What is a plausible explanation?

**Answer.** The proposal steps are too small ($\sigma_{\text{prop}} \approx 0$). The chain accepts almost every proposal but explores the posterior only by slow local diffusion, generating heavily autocorrelated draws.
````
