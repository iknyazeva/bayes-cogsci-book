> **Organizing Question:** What do imputation and poststratification adjust—and what do they fail to automatically repair when generalizing from sample to population?

Empirical datasets rarely represent the exact population of scientific interest. In psychological experiments, university student participant pools over-represent educated, high-income youths. In public opinion and cognitive epidemiology, survey respondents systematically refuse to disclose income or skip difficult test questions.

Two foundational Bayesian methodologies address these discrepancies:
1. **Bayesian Multiple Imputation**: Estimating missing values inside the joint posterior distribution under declared missingness mechanisms.
2. **Multilevel Regression and Poststratification (MRP)**: Correcting sample selection imbalances by reweighting model-based estimates to match census or population demographic tables.

## 1. Taxonomy of Missing Data Mechanisms

Following Little & Rubin (2002), missing data mechanisms are defined by the probability of an observation being missing ($M_i \in \{0, 1\}$):

| Mechanism | Formal Definition | Substantive Meaning | Complete-Case Analysis Valid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MCAR** (Missing Completely at Random) | $P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, Y_{\text{mis}}, X) = P(M)$ | Missingness is pure coin-flip noise unrelated to any variable | Valid, but inefficient (throws away data) |
| **MAR** (Missing at Random) | $P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, Y_{\text{mis}}, X) = P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, X)$ | Missingness depends on *observed* covariates, but not on the unobserved value | **Biased** if rows are deleted; valid under joint imputation |
| **MNAR** (Missing Not at Random) | $P(M \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, Y_{\text{mis}}, X)$ depends on $Y_{\text{mis}}$ | Missingness directly depends on the unobserved missing value itself | **Severely Biased**; requires explicit selection modeling |

```{warning}
**Why "Complete-Case Analysis" (Listwise Deletion) Causes Bias:**
Deleting all rows with missing values implicitly conditions on the missingness indicator ($M_i = 0$). If missingness depends on covariates or outcomes, listwise deletion acts as **collider stratification**, inducing artificial associations and severe sample selection bias!
```

## 2. Bayesian Imputation in PyMC

In Bayesian workflow, unknown missing values $y_{\text{mis}}$ are simply treated as **unobserved parameters** to be estimated simultaneously with model coefficients:

```python
import pymc as pm
import numpy as np

# Mask missing values as NaN in NumPy array
y_with_missing = y_data.copy()

with pm.Model() as imputation_model:
    alpha = pm.Normal("alpha", mu=0, sigma=10)
    beta = pm.Normal("beta", mu=0, sigma=5)
    sigma = pm.HalfNormal("sigma", sigma=5)
    
    # Linear model
    mu = alpha + beta * x_predictor
    
    # PyMC automatically creates an unobserved variable for missing entries
    y_obs = pm.Normal("y", mu=mu, sigma=sigma, observed=y_with_missing)
    
    idata_imp = pm.sample(draws=1000, tune=1000)
```

## 3. Multilevel Regression and Poststratification (MRP)

When a sample is unrepresentative of the target population (e.g. 80% female respondents in an online survey), raw sample averages are misleading. **MRP** (Gelman & Little, 1997) repairs this in two steps:

### Step 1: Multilevel Regression (Estimation)
We partition the population into demographic/geographic cells $c$ (e.g. 4 age brackets $\times$ 2 sexes $\times$ 3 education levels = 24 cells). We fit a hierarchical model estimating cell probabilities:

$$
\theta_c = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\alpha + u_{\text{age}[c]} + v_{\text{sex}[c]} + w_{\text{edu}[c]})
$$

Partial pooling shares information across small or empty demographic cells, providing regularized, stable cell estimates.

### Step 2: Poststratification (Weighting to Census Target)
From census or demographic data, we know the exact true population counts $N_c$ and proportions $w_c = \frac{N_c}{N_{\text{pop}}}$. We aggregate the cell predictions to compute the population estimate:

$$
\theta_{\text{pop}} = \sum_{c=1}^C w_c \theta_c
$$

Because this is performed on every posterior draw, we obtain the **full posterior distribution of the population estimand**!

## 4. Empirical Case Study: Polling & Social Indicators Survey

### Anchor Model Card: Poststratified Preference Estimation

| Field | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Sample Data** | Non-representative survey sample ($N = 1{,}200$) with sex, age, and education cells |
| **Target Population** | General adult population ($N_{\text{census}} = 50{,}000$) |
| **Primary Estimand** | Population agreement proportion: $\theta_{\text{pop}} = \sum_c w_c \theta_c$ |
| **Model** | Hierarchical logistic regression with varying intercepts for demographic cells |
| **Priors** | Weakly informative $\operatorname{Normal}(0, 1)$ on grand mean and $\operatorname{Exponential}(1)$ on cell scales |
| **Adjustment Limit** | Cannot repair within-cell selection (unmeasured behavioral traits) |

---

## 5. What Poststratification Does Not Repair

```{caution}
**The Limits of MRP:**
Poststratification adjusts for **observed cell composition imbalances**. It does **not** correct:
1. **Within-cell selection bias**: If the 20-year-olds who choose to take your survey think differently from the 20-year-olds who decline, MRP cannot remove that bias.
2. **Measurement Error**: If respondents misreport their attitudes or income.
3. **Omitted Demographic Dimensions**: If an important determinant of the outcome is absent from both the survey and the census tables.
```

````{dropdown} Concept Check: The Fallacy of the "Representative Sample"
A market research firm claims: "Our online sample was poststratified on age and gender, so it is now fully representative of the country." What critical assumption is violated?

**Answer.** Poststratification ensures that the *proportions* of age and gender in the reweighted estimate match the census. However, it assumes that within every age-by-gender cell, respondents who opt into an online survey are identical to those who do not (ignorability of selection within cells). Online survey opt-ins often have higher tech literacy, leisure time, or distinct political views. Poststratification cannot repair unmeasured within-cell selection bias.
````

## Key Takeaways

- Deleting missing rows (complete-case analysis) discards statistical power and induces severe selection bias when missingness is MAR or MNAR.
- In Bayesian workflow, missing values are unobserved parameters integrated directly into the posterior.
- **Multilevel Regression and Poststratification (MRP)** combines hierarchical shrinkage across demographic cells with census weighting to generalize to target populations.
- Poststratification corrects for observable demographic imbalances, but cannot fix within-cell selection bias.
