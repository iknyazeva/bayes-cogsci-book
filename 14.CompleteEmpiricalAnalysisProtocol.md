> **Organizing Question:** How do we synthesize the entire Bayesian workflow into an end-to-end, reproducible research protocol and oral defence for empirical science?

In this capstone session, we integrate all foundational (Sessions 1–6), applied (Sessions 7–10), and evaluative (Sessions 11–13) methods into a **complete, reproducible empirical analysis package**.

The goal of Bayesian empirical analysis is not to maximize model complexity or write hundreds of lines of obscure code. A simple, transparent, well-calibrated model with rigorous diagnostics and honest limitations will always score higher than an uncalibrated, overfitted neural network or an uninterpretable hierarchical model with hidden divergences.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       THE 16-POINT REPRODUCIBLE RESEARCH PROTOCOL                             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. Scientific Question & Construct Operationalization                                        |
|  2. Observational Unit & Declared Target Population                                           |
|  3. Primary Estimand (Descriptive, Predictive, or Causal)                                     |
```

## 1. The Capstone Model Card Template

Every completed project must provide an executive **Model Card** summarizing the statistical system:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Model Name** | Concise, descriptive model designation |
| **Research Question** | One sentence stating the empirical question |
| **Observational Unit** | The individual experimental trial, participant, or household |
| **Target Population** | The declared universe of units to which inference applies |
| **Estimand** | Mathematical expression of the primary quantity on the outcome scale |
| **Likelihood & Link** | Formal probability distribution ($y_i \sim p(y \mid \mu_i)$) and link $g(\mu)$ |
| **Priors** | Weakly informative, scale-aware priors in observable measurement units |
| **Hierarchy** | Clear specification of varying intercepts and slopes across grouping factors |
| **Diagnostic Status** | Numerical confirmation of passing the Computational Gate |
| **Targeted PPC** | Name of test statistic $T(y)$ and resulting Bayesian $p$-value |
| **Limitations** | Statement of untestable causal and selection assumptions |

## 2. Project Evaluation Rubric (Common Task Rubric)

Submissions are evaluated across five distinct dimensions (each scored from 0 to 4):

| Dimension | Core Standard for Excellence (Score 4) | Common Failure (Score 1–2) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Scientific Framing & Estimands** | Clear substantive question; explicit observational unit; estimand defined mathematically on the natural outcome scale. | Vague question; coefficients interpreted in isolation without an estimand. |
| **2. Generative Modeling & Priors** | Likelihood justified by measurement support; weakly informative priors verified via prior predictive simulation. | Default uncalibrated priors; prior predictive checks omitted or producing impossible values. |
| **3. Computational Rigor** | Zero divergences; rank-normalized split $\widehat{R} \le 1.01$; bulk and tail $\text{ESS} \ge 400$; parameterization justified. | Sampler run with divergences or non-convergence; diagnostics ignored. |
| **4. Predictive Checking & Comparison** | Targeted PPC audits critical data features; PSIS-LOO compares models using the correct prediction unit (e.g. grouped units). | Generic histogram overlay only; LOO used mechanically as an "automatic truth detector." |
| **5. Honest Limits & Reproducibility** | Explicit non-causal boundaries; missingness/selection limits declared; fully reproducible pipeline from raw data to final report. | Causal claims made without DAG identification; unweighted sample claimed as "representative." |

## 3. Oral Defence Preparation Checklist

In your short oral defence, be prepared to answer five focused questions:
1. *Why was this likelihood chosen, and what physical or behavioral process justifies its support?*
2. *How did you choose your priors, and what would happen if their scale were doubled or halved?*
3. *What diagnostic evidence proves that the MCMC sampler explored the posterior distribution reliably?*
4. *Where does your fitted model show its greatest predictive discrepancy in PPCs?*
5. *Under what circumstances would your primary conclusion fail to generalize or fail to be causal?*

````{dropdown} Concept Check: The Role of Complexity in Grading
Does a hierarchical model with 4-way interactions and a custom mixture likelihood receive a higher grade than a well-justified linear regression with proper centering and diagnostics?

**Answer.** Absolutely not. Course grading explicitly penalizes unneeded complexity. A simpler model whose parameters are substantively meaningful, whose priors are calibrated, whose diagnostics pass cleanly, and whose limitations are thoroughly understood will consistently score higher than an overly complex model with uninterpretable parameters and fragile computation.
````

## Key Takeaways

- An empirical analysis is evaluated by its **transparency, calibration, diagnostic rigor, and honest boundaries**, not by code complexity.
- Complete the **16-Point Empirical Research Protocol** as a cumulative contract.
- Always include an executive **Model Card** summarizing the statistical system.
- Ensure the analysis pipeline runs reproducibly from raw data to final figures in a clean environment.
