## Bags and Marbles

For Questions 5-9, use the following common task to practice conditional probability.

**Task Description:**

You have three bags labeled A, B, and C, each containing different quantities of red and blue marbles. The contents of each bag are as follows:

- **Bag A:** Contains 2 red marbles and 3 blue marbles.
- **Bag B:** Contains 5 red marbles and 1 blue marble.
- **Bag C:** Contains only 3 red marbles (no blue marbles).


**Question 5** If you select from bag B, what is the probability that you will draw a red marble? Express the exact answer as a simplified fraction.


**Question 6** If you randomly select one of the three bags with equal probability (so that $P(A)=P(B)=P(C)=1/3$) and then randomly draw a marble from that bag, what is the probability that the marble will be blue? Round your answer to two decimal places.


**Question 7** Suppose a bag is randomly selected (again, with equal probability), but you do not know which it is. You randomly draw a marble and observe that it is blue. What is the probability that the bag you selected this marble from is A? That is, find $P(A \mid \text{blue})$. Round your answer to two decimal places.


**Question 8** Suppose a bag is randomly selected (again, with equal probability), but you do not know which it is. You randomly draw a marble and observe that it is blue. What is the probability that the bag you selected from is C? That is, find $P(C \mid \text{blue})$. Round your answer to two decimal places.

**Question 9** Suppose a bag is randomly selected (again, with equal probability), but you do not know which it is. You randomly draw a marble and observe that it is red. What is the probability that the bag you selected from is C? That is, find $P(C \mid \text{red})$. Round your answer to two decimal places.


## Cognitive Screening

A neuropsychologist uses a brief cognitive screening tool (such as the MoCA) in a memory clinic. Based on published validation studies:

- The test correctly identifies patients with cognitive impairment 85% of the time (sensitivity = 0.85).
- The test correctly identifies healthy individuals 90% of the time (specificity = 0.90).
- In the clinic's population, 20% of patients actually have cognitive impairment (prevalence = 0.20).

**Question 10** What is the probability that a patient who tests positive actually has cognitive impairment? Apply Bayes' theorem. Round your answer to two decimal places.

**Question 11** What is the probability that a patient who tests negative is truly healthy? Round your answer to two decimal places.

**Question 12** If the prevalence in a general population screening is only 5% instead of 20%, how does $P(\text{impairment} \mid +)$ change? What does this tell us about the importance of base rates in clinical decision-making?
