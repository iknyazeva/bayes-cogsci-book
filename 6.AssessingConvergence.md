# Assessing Convergence & MCMC Diagnostics

Before interpreting posterior draws from an MCMC sampler or drawing scientific conclusions, we must pass the **Diagnostic Gate**:

> **Has our simulated Markov chain reliably explored the entire stationary target distribution without computational pathologies?**

```{mermaid}
flowchart TD
    A["Run MCMC Sampling (PyMC 5)"] --> B{"Check Divergences"}
    B -- "> 0 Divergences" --> C["❌ Pathological Geometry<br>Reparameterize / Increase target_accept"]
    B -- "0 Divergences" --> D{"Check Rank-Normalized R-hat"}
    D -- "R-hat > 1.01" --> E["❌ Chains Haven't Mixed<br>Check priors / run longer chains"]
    D -- "R-hat ≤ 1.01" --> F{"Check Bulk & Tail ESS"}
    F -- "ESS < 400" --> G["⚠️ High Autocorrelation<br>Insufficient effective sample size"]
    F -- "ESS ≥ 400" --> H["✅ Pass Diagnostic Gate<br>Proceed to Posterior Interpretation"]
```

---

## 1. Visual Diagnostic: The Trace Plot & Rank Plots

A **trace plot** displays the sequential trajectory of sampled parameter values across iterations for each parallel chain.

### What to Look For:
1. **Stationarity ("Fuzzy Caterpillar")**: The chains should quickly fluctuate rapidly around a constant mean level without upward or downward drift.
2. **Chain Mixing**: Multiple independent chains starting from dispersed initial values must overlap seamlessly throughout the sampling phase.
3. **Rank Plots**: Modern rank histograms should appear approximately uniform across all chains. Non-uniform histograms indicate that one chain was exploring different regions than the others.

---

## 2. Autocorrelation & Effective Sample Size (ESS)

Because MCMC algorithms generate dependent samples where $\theta^{(t+1)}$ depends on $\theta^{(t)}$, successive draws exhibit **autocorrelation**:

$$\rho_k = \frac{\text{Cov}(\theta^{(t)}, \theta^{(t+k)})}{\text{Var}(\theta)}$$

### Bulk vs. Tail Effective Sample Size
Modern Bayesian diagnostics (Vehtari et al., 2021) distinguish between:
* **$ESS_{\text{bulk}}$**: Measures the effective sample size for estimating central tendencies (mean, median).
* **$ESS_{\text{tail}}$**: Measures the effective sample size for estimating tail quantiles (e.g. $2.5\%$ and $97.5\%$ credible interval bounds).

> **Course Threshold**: Both $ESS_{\text{bulk}}$ and $ESS_{\text{tail}}$ should be at least **$400$** (100 per chain for 4 chains) to ensure reliable mean and interval estimates.

### Why Thinning is Usually Unnecessary
In early MCMC practice, researchers often "thinned" chains by discarding every $k$-th sample to reduce autocorrelation. In modern computing:
* Discarding valid samples never improves the precision of posterior summaries—it throws away information.
* **Thinning is only recommended if disk storage or memory is severely constrained.**

---

## 3. Rank-Normalized Split $\hat{R}$ (Gelman-Rubin Metric)

The modern **rank-normalized split $\hat{R}$** (Vehtari et al., 2021) compares the variance *between* multiple chains to the variance *within* chains, after splitting each chain in half to detect non-stationarity within chains:

$$\hat{R} = \sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\text{Var}}^+(\theta)}{W}}$$

Where:
* $W$ is the average within-chain variance.
* $\widehat{\text{Var}}^+(\theta) = \frac{N-1}{N}W + \frac{1}{N}B$ is the combined estimate of the total marginal variance.
* Values are computed on rank-transformed draws to handle heavy-tailed distributions and asymmetric boundaries robustly.

> **Course Threshold**: $\hat{R} \le 1.01$. Values $\hat{R} > 1.01$ indicate that chains have not converged to a common stationary distribution and the model cannot be interpreted.

---

## 4. Hamiltonian Monte Carlo & NUTS Diagnostics

When using modern gradient-based samplers like the No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS) in **PyMC 5**:

1. **Divergences**:
   - A divergence occurs when the numerical leapfrog integrator encounters regions of high posterior curvature (e.g. Neal's Funnel) and cannot follow Hamiltonian trajectories accurately.
   - **Rule**: A single divergence indicates that part of the posterior geometry is unexplored. Solutions: increase `target_accept` (e.g. from 0.8 to 0.95) or reparameterize the model (e.g. non-centered parameterization).
2. **Energy Bayesian Fraction of Missing Information (E-BFMI)**:
   - Evaluates the efficiency of momentum sampling. Values below $0.3$ suggest sluggish energy transitions.

---

## Summary: The Course Diagnostic Gate Checklist

| Metric | Target / Passing Threshold | Action if Failed |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Divergences** | **0 divergences** | Increase `target_accept` or switch to non-centered parameterization. |
| **Split $\hat{R}$** | **$\hat{R} \le 1.01$** | Check for multimodality, tighten priors, or increase warmup iterations. |
| **$ESS_{\text{bulk}}$** | **$\ge 400$** | Increase number of sampling draws or reparameterize correlated variables. |
| **$ESS_{\text{tail}}$** | **$\ge 400$** | Increase sampling draws to reliably resolve interval boundaries. |
