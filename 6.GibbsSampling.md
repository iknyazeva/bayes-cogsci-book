# Part 6.4: Gibbs sampling

Gibbs sampling constructs a Markov chain by drawing each parameter, or block of parameters, from its full conditional distribution. It is a special case of Metropolis–Hastings in which the proposed conditional draw is accepted with probability one.

## 1. Full conditional distributions

For a joint posterior $p(\theta_1,\theta_2\mid y)$, the full conditionals are

$$
p(\theta_1\mid\theta_2,y)
\quad\text{and}\quad
p(\theta_2\mid\theta_1,y).
$$

A two-block Gibbs sweep is

$$
\theta_1^{(s)}\sim p(\theta_1\mid\theta_2^{(s-1)},y),
$$

followed by

$$
\theta_2^{(s)}\sim p(\theta_2\mid\theta_1^{(s)},y).
$$

The second update uses the newly drawn value of $\theta_1$. After both updates, one sweep is complete.

Pure Gibbs sampling should not be described as proposing an arbitrary value and then applying a separate accept–reject decision. When a full conditional cannot be sampled directly, a Metropolis step may be placed inside a Gibbs schedule; that is a **Metropolis-within-Gibbs** algorithm.

## 2. Geometric intuition

For a bivariate Normal target, drawing $x\mid y$ moves horizontally and drawing $y\mid x$ moves vertically. If the posterior correlation is weak, these alternating moves cover the joint distribution quickly. If it is strong, the path advances slowly along a narrow diagonal ridge.

## 3. Worked Normal model with independent priors

Let

$$
y_i\mid\mu,\sigma^2\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2),
$$

with independent priors

$$
\mu\sim\mathcal N(\mu_0,\tau^2),
\qquad
\sigma^2\sim\operatorname{InvGamma}(\alpha_0,\beta_0).
$$

The joint posterior kernel is

$$
p(\mu,\sigma^2\mid y)
\propto
(\sigma^2)^{-n/2}
\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\mu)^2\right]
\exp\left[-\frac{(\mu-\mu_0)^2}{2\tau^2}\right]
(\sigma^2)^{-(\alpha_0+1)}
\exp\left(-\frac{\beta_0}{\sigma^2}\right).
$$

Holding $\sigma^2$ fixed gives

$$
\mu\mid\sigma^2,y
\sim
\mathcal N(m_n,V_n),
$$

where

$$
V_n=\left(\frac{n}{\sigma^2}+\frac{1}{\tau^2}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
m_n=V_n\left(\frac{n\bar y}{\sigma^2}+\frac{\mu_0}{\tau^2}\right).
$$

Holding $\mu$ fixed gives

$$
\sigma^2\mid\mu,y
\sim
\operatorname{InvGamma}\left(
\alpha_0+\frac n2,
\beta_0+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\mu)^2
\right).
$$

These formulas assume the stated **independent** prior. They should not be silently combined with the conditionally conjugate Normal–inverse-Gamma prior $\mu\mid\sigma^2\sim\mathcal N(\mu_0,\sigma^2/\kappa_0)$, whose updates differ.

## 4. A minimal Gibbs implementation

```python
mu = np.empty(draws)
sigma2 = np.empty(draws)
mu[0] = y.mean()
sigma2[0] = y.var(ddof=1)

for s in range(1, draws):
    V = 1 / (n / sigma2[s - 1] + 1 / tau2)
    m = V * (n * y.mean() / sigma2[s - 1] + mu0 / tau2)
    mu[s] = rng.normal(m, np.sqrt(V))

    shape = alpha0 + n / 2
    scale = beta0 + 0.5 * np.sum((y - mu[s]) ** 2)
    precision = rng.gamma(shape=shape, scale=1 / scale)
    sigma2[s] = 1 / precision
```

The last two lines simulate an inverse-Gamma variable via its reciprocal Gamma precision. This makes the NumPy rate/scale conversion explicit.

## 5. When Gibbs sampling is useful

Gibbs sampling remains valuable when:

- full conditionals belong to standard distributions;
- a model contains discrete latent states that gradient methods cannot update directly;
- conjugate blocks can be updated efficiently;
- the algorithm is used to understand the structure of a joint posterior.

For continuous differentiable models in PyMC, NUTS is usually preferred because it updates parameters jointly using gradients. Some models combine several step methods, but the resulting schedule and diagnostics must be documented.

## 6. Blocking and reparameterisation

Component-wise updates can be slow when parameters are strongly dependent. Two main remedies are:

- **blocking:** draw a correlated vector jointly from a multivariate full conditional;
- **reparameterisation:** express the model in coordinates with weaker posterior dependence.

More iterations reduce MCSE only if the chain actually explores the relevant distribution. They do not remove a geometric bottleneck.

## 8. Guided practice

````{dropdown} Question 1: 100% acceptance with slow exploration
Why can a Gibbs sampler mix slowly even though every conditional draw is accepted?

**Answer.** Acceptance does not measure distance travelled through the joint distribution. With strong posterior dependence, alternating one-coordinate updates can move only gradually along a narrow ridge, creating severe autocorrelation.
````

````{dropdown} Question 2: Non-standard full conditionals
What changes when one full conditional is unavailable in a standard form?

**Answer.** That block can be updated with another valid transition, such as Metropolis–Hastings, producing a Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler. Its proposal and acceptance diagnostics must then be assessed as well.
````
