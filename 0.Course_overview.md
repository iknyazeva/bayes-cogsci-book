# Bayesian Analysis of Empirical Data: Course Overview

## 1. Course Rationale & Scope

Welcome to **Bayesian Analysis of Empirical Data**. This course develops Bayesian statistics as a rigorous, practical language for modeling uncertainty, preserving multilevel structure, and drawing principled inferences from empirical data across the social, cognitive, and behavioural sciences.

Rather than treating Bayesian methods as a collection of isolated statistical tests, the curriculum is organized around a complete generative modeling workflow: from substantive question formulation and prior elicitation to computational sampling, diagnostics, predictive checking, causal boundary analysis, and reproducible research protocols.

## 2. The 14-Session Curriculum Map

The curriculum consists of **14 four-hour sessions (56 contact academic hours)**. Each session connects a methodological principle with an anchor empirical case:

| Session | Core Topic | Anchor Empirical Case | Primary Computational / Interactive Asset |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Research questions, estimands & measurement** | Survey response & cognitive indicators (*ROS* Ch. 2; BRFSS) | Measurement error simulator |
| **2** | **Probability & Bayesian updating** | Diagnostic screening & base rates (*Kruschke* Ch. 2; *Jackman* Ch. 1) | Base-rate & probability tree explorer |
| **3** | **Generative models, likelihood & prediction** | Trial accuracy & simulated polling (*McElreath* Ch. 2–3) | Generative data-process simulator |
| **4** | **Priors, posterior uncertainty & checks** | Beta-Binomial policy support updating (*ROS* Ch. 4; *Kruschke* Ch. 5) | Interactive Prior–Likelihood–Posterior dashboard |
| **5** | **Simulation, parameter recovery & design** | Educational intervention evaluation (*ROS* Ch. 5 & 16) | Fake-data parameter recovery lab |
| **6** | **Bayesian computation (MCMC, HMC, NUTS)** | 1D/2D random walk & PyMC baseline (*Jackman* Ch. 3; *McElreath*) | Interactive Metropolis-Hastings visual sampler |
| **7** | **Continuous outcomes & robust models** | Reaction times, earnings & Newcomb light speed (*ROS* Ch. 11; *Maier et al.*) | Normal vs. Student-$t$ outlier comparison |
| **8** | **Regression, interactions & prediction** | KidIQ cognitive development & earnings (*ROS* Ch. 6–10) | Interactive regression & interaction surface |
| **9** | **Generalized linear models (GLMs)** | Bangladesh well switching, cockroach counts, ordinal attitudes (*ROS* Ch. 13–15; *Jackman* Ch. 8) | Inverse link & threshold visualizer |

| **12** | **Causal inference & DAGs** | Electric Company & Sesame Street evaluations (*ROS* Ch. 18–20) | Causal collider / confounder simulator |
| **13** | **Missing data & poststratification** | Social Indicators Survey & Xbox election polling (*ROS* Ch. 17) | Poststratification & selection adjustment lab |
| **14** | **Complete empirical research protocol** | Cognitive & sociological capstone analyses | Guided research protocol builder & checklist |

## 3. How to Use Each Chapter

Every applied chapter follows a standard 5-step learning cadence:

$$\text{Concept} \longrightarrow \text{Streamlit Demo} \longrightarrow \text{Worked Example} \longrightarrow \text{Laboratory Notebook} \longrightarrow \text{Protocol Task}$$

1. **Concept**: Substantive research question, target estimand, and explicit generative assumptions.
2. **Interactive Demonstration**: Visual exploration using fast analytical curves or lightweight simulations to build mathematical intuition before running heavy samplers.
3. **Worked Example**: Complete model implementation using **PyMC 5**, **Bambi**, and **ArviZ** with code walkthrough.
4. **Laboratory Notebook**: An executable practical notebook where students fit models, evaluate convergence gates, and generate observable predictions.
5. **Cumulative Protocol Task**: A concrete task where students document one component (e.g. priors, DAG, diagnostics, or sensitivity analysis) for their own final research project.

## 4. Software & Platform Policy

### Computational Environment
* **Primary Stack**: Python 3.12, **PyMC 5**, **ArviZ**, **Bambi**, **NumPy**, **SciPy**, **Matplotlib**, and **Plotly**.
* **Optional Google Colab**: Computational laboratory notebooks include launch links to Google Colab as an optional browser-based environment, subject to platform availability and session resource limits. Conceptual reading chapters are published directly as static, fast-loading web pages.
* **Streamlit Interactive Platform**: In-development interactive demonstrations are located under `streamlit_app/` in the course repository. They provide zero-friction visual controls for prior updating, MCMC step mechanics, and multilevel shrinkage.

### AI and RAG Policy
* **Student Requirements**: Students do **not** need access to AI systems, large language models, or Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) tools to complete the course readings, exercises, or research projects.
* **Instructor-Side Quality Control**: RAG validation is used exclusively as an internal, instructor-side authoring tool to audit factual citations, case provenance, and textbook attributions.
