> **Organizing Question:** Why can a regression coefficient never be called causal without explicit identifying assumptions, and how do graphical causal models guide statistical adjustment?

In Sessions 7–10, we emphasized that regression estimates conditional expectations $\mathbb{E}[y \mid x]$, not causal effects. Observing that children whose mothers graduated from high school score higher on cognitive tests does not prove that forcing graduation will increase a child's score by $\beta_{\text{hs}}$. 

Causal inference requires a mathematical framework to bridge the gap between **passive statistical observation** ($p(y \mid x)$) and **active experimental intervention** ($p(y \mid \operatorname{do}(X = x))$).

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               THE TRIAD OF CAUSAL REASONING                                   |
+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+
|  1. Potential Outcomes        |  2. Structural DAGs           |  3. Identification Gate       |
|                               |                               |                               |
|  Y(1) - Y(0)                  |  Confounders (Forks): X ← Z → Y|  Ignorability: Y(t) ⫫ T | X  |
|  Average Treatment Effect     |  Mediators (Chains):  X → M → Y|  Positivity: 0 < P(T=1|X) < 1 |
|  Intention-to-Treat (ITT)     |  Colliders:           X → C ← Y|  Consistency: Y = Y(T_obs)   |
+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+
```

## 1. Potential Outcomes & Causal Estimands

Following Neyman and Rubin, for every unit $i$ exposed to binary treatment $T_i \in \{0, 1\}$, there exist two **potential outcomes**:
- $Y_i(1)$: The outcome unit $i$ would experience under treatment.
- $Y_i(0)$: The outcome unit $i$ would experience under control.

The **individual causal effect** is $\tau_i = Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)$. 

### The Fundamental Problem of Causal Inference
Because an individual cannot simultaneously receive treatment and control, we only observe:

$$
Y_i^{\text{obs}} = T_i Y_i(1) + (1 - T_i) Y_i(0)
$$

The counterfactual outcome is always **missing data**! Causal inference therefore targets population averages:
1. **Average Treatment Effect (ATE)**: $\tau_{\text{ATE}} = \mathbb{E}[Y(1) - Y(0)]$.
2. **Average Treatment Effect on the Treated (ATT)**: $\tau_{\text{ATT}} = \mathbb{E}[Y(1) - Y(0) \mid T = 1]$.
3. **Intention-to-Treat (ITT)**: In randomized trials with non-compliance, the effect of treatment assignment rather than actual uptake.

## 2. Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) and Elemental Confounders

Directed Acyclic Graphs (Pearl, 2009) encode our qualitative, substantive scientific assumptions about the data-generating mechanism.

### The Three Elemental Causal Structures:

```
    Confounder (Fork)               Mediator (Chain)               Collider (Inverted Fork)
       Z (Adjust!)                     M (Do Not Adjust!)             C (Do NOT Adjust!)
      ↙ ↘                             ↗ ↘                             ↘ ↙
     X   Y                           X   Y                           X   Y
   Spurious Association            Transmits Causal Effect         Stratification Bias!
```

1. **The Fork (Confounder $X \leftarrow Z \rightarrow Y$)**:
   - Variable $Z$ influences both treatment $X$ and outcome $Y$.
   - Failing to condition on $Z$ creates an open backdoor path, confounding the relationship.
   - **Remedy**: Condition / adjust for $Z$.
2. **The Pipe / Chain (Mediator $X \rightarrow M \rightarrow Y$)**:
   - Variable $M$ lies along the causal pathway from $X$ to $Y$.
   - Conditioning on $M$ blocks the mechanism and eliminates the indirect causal effect.
   - **Remedy**: Do **not** adjust for $M$ when estimating the total causal effect!
3. **The Inverted Fork (Collider $X \rightarrow C \leftarrow Y$)**:
   - Both treatment $X$ and outcome $Y$ cause collider $C$.
   - Marginally, $X$ and $Y$ are independent ($X \perp\!\!\perp Y$).
   - **Conditioning on $C$ artificially opens a spurious association between $X$ and $Y$!**
   - **Remedy**: Strictly avoid adjusting for colliders.

```{warning}
**Collider Stratification Bias in Cognitive Science:**
Testing only university undergraduates conditions on university admission (a collider between conscientiousness and cognitive ability). This routinely induces spurious negative correlations between traits that are positively correlated in the general population!
```

## 4. The Three Untestable Assumptions for Observational Identification

When physical randomization is absent, identifying causal effects from observational regression requires:
1. **Conditional Exchangeability / Ignorability**: $\{Y(0), Y(1)\} \perp\!\!\perp T \mid X$. Given observed covariates $X$, treatment assignment is as good as random.
2. **Positivity / Common Support**: $0 < P(T = 1 \mid X = x) < 1$ for all valid $x$. Every covariate profile has a non-zero probability of receiving both treatment and control.
3. **Consistency**: $Y = Y(T)$ with no hidden variations of treatment or interference between units (SUTVA).

```{important}
**Bayesian Estimation Cannot Cure Non-Identification:**
MCMC provides exact posterior uncertainty given the model. But if an unobserved confounder $U$ exists ($X \leftarrow U \rightarrow Y$), no amount of MCMC sampling or fancy priors will identify the true causal effect!
```

````{dropdown} Concept Check: The Post-Treatment Variable Trap
A researcher measures whether a new cognitive training app ($T$) increases exam scores ($Y$). They include "daily app usage hours" ($M$) as a covariate in the regression. What happens to the estimated effect of $T$?

**Answer.** Daily app usage is a **mediator** lying directly on the causal chain ($T \rightarrow M \rightarrow Y$). Controlling for $M$ blocks the causal mechanism and leaves only the direct effect (if any), masking the true total benefit of the training program. To estimate total causal effects, never condition on post-treatment variables.
````

## Key Takeaways

- Regression coefficients measure statistical association; causal effects represent the consequences of **hypothetical interventions** ($p(y \mid \operatorname{do}(x))$).
- Use **DAGs** to encode domain assumptions and select valid backdoor adjustment sets.
- Never adjust for **mediators** when estimating total effects, and never adjust for **colliders** (which induces Berkson's / selection bias).
- Bayesian computation quantifies estimation uncertainty, not identification validity.
