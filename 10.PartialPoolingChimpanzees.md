# Partial Pooling & The Prosocial Chimpanzees Case Study

## 1. The Three Approaches to Multi-Group Data

When analyzing data collected across multiple individuals, subjects, or experimental clusters ($j = 1, \dots, J$), researchers must choose how to treat individual differences:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             THE THREE POOLING PARADIGMS                                       |
+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+
|  1. Complete Pooling          |  2. No Pooling                |  3. Partial Pooling (Hier.)   |
|                               |                               |                               |
|  All individuals share a single|  Each individual estimated as |  Individuals drawn from a     |
|  common parameter: αⱼ = α     |  independent fixed parameter  |  common population prior:     |
|  ❌ Underfits (ignores variation)|  ❌ Overfits (extreme estimates)|  αⱼ ~ Normal(μ_α, σ_α)        |
|                               |                               |  ✅ Optimal bias-variance     |
+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+-------------------------------+
```

## 2. Empirical Case Study: Prosocial Chimpanzees

In a famous experiment by Silk et al. (2005) analyzed in Richard McElreath's *Statistical Rethinking* (Ch. 11 & 13), $N = 7$ focal chimpanzee actors participated in an experimental task with two levers:
- **Prosocial Option (1/1)**: Delivers a food treat to both the actor and a partner in the adjacent cage.
- **Selfish Option (1/0)**: Delivers a food treat only to the actor.

The experiment manipulated two factors across 504 total trials:
1. `prosoc_left` $\in \{0, 1\}$: Whether the prosocial food dish was positioned on the left lever.
2. `condition` $\in \{0, 1\}$: Whether a partner was present in the adjacent cage ($1$) or absent ($0$, control).

The binary response is $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$: whether the chimpanzee pulled the left lever.

## 3. PyMC Implementation: From No-Pooling to Hierarchical Shrinkage

### Model 1: No Pooling (Independent Fixed Intercepts)
In a No-Pooling model, each actor $j \in \{0, \dots, 6\}$ receives an independent unconstrained prior:

```python
with pm.Model() as model_no_pooling:
    # 7 independent fixed intercepts
    alpha_actor = pm.Normal("alpha_actor", mu=0.0, sigma=10.0, shape=7)
    beta_treatment = pm.Normal("beta_treatment", mu=0.0, sigma=0.5, shape=4)
    
    # Linear predictor
    p = pm.Deterministic("p", pm.math.invlogit(alpha_actor[actor_id] + beta_treatment[treatment_id]))
    
    # Likelihood
    pm.Bernoulli("y_obs", p=p, observed=pulled_left)
    idata_no_pool = pm.sample(draws=1000, tune=1000)
```
*Problem*: Actor 2 pulled left on 90 out of 90 trials ($100\%$). Under unregularized priors, $\alpha_2 \to +\infty$ on the logit scale, causing numerical divergence and infinite predictive variance.

### Model 2: Partial Pooling (Hierarchical Non-Centered Model)
In the Hierarchical Bayesian model, individual intercepts $\alpha_j$ are drawn from a shared population distribution $\operatorname{Normal}(\bar{\alpha}, \sigma_\alpha)$:

```python
with pm.Model() as model_partial_pooling:
    # Hyperpriors for population mean and between-chimp variation
    a_bar = pm.Normal("a_bar", mu=0.0, sigma=1.5)
    sigma_a = pm.Exponential("sigma_a", lam=1.0)
    
    # Non-centered individual actor offsets
    z_actor = pm.Normal("z_actor", mu=0.0, sigma=1.0, shape=7)
    alpha_actor = pm.Deterministic("alpha_actor", a_bar + sigma_a * z_actor)
    
    # Treatment effects (4 combinations: prosoc_left x condition)
    beta_treatment = pm.Normal("beta_treatment", mu=0.0, sigma=0.5, shape=4)
    
    # Likelihood
    p = pm.Deterministic("p", pm.math.invlogit(alpha_actor[actor_id] + beta_treatment[treatment_id]))
    pm.Bernoulli("y_obs", p=p, observed=pulled_left)
    
    idata_partial = pm.sample(draws=2000, tune=1000, target_accept=0.95)
```

## 4. Substantive Conclusions from the Chimpanzee Analysis

1. **Massive Handedness / Personality Heterogeneity**: Between-chimpanzee variation ($\sigma_\alpha \approx 1.3$) dwarfs experimental treatment effects ($\beta \approx 0.15$). Actor 2 is strongly left-handed, while Actor 1 and 3 are strongly right-handed.
2. **Absence of Prosocial Intent**: After controlling for individual handedness via partial pooling, the presence of a partner in the adjacent cage ($\text{condition}=1$) did **not** increase the probability of pulling the prosocial lever ($\beta_{\text{partner}} \approx 0.05 \pm 0.18$, 95% HDI overlaps zero).
3. **Adaptive Shrinkage in Action**: Partial pooling prevents Actor 2's $100\%$ left-pull rate from exploding to infinity, shrinking the estimated latent intercept from $\alpha_2 = +\infty$ down to a well-behaved posterior mean of $+3.2$ ($\approx 96\%$ on the probability scale).

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Complete Pooling** underfits by ignoring real individual differences.
- **No Pooling** overfits by treating small sample clusters as isolated universes, blowing up extreme individuals.
- **Partial Pooling** provides adaptive regularization: it lets each individual have their own personality while learning the population variance $\sigma_\alpha$ from all clusters simultaneously.
