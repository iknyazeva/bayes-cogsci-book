# Step-by-Step Case Study: Prosocial Chimpanzees and Pooling Paradigms

## 1. Experimental Setup & Task Description

In a famous evolutionary anthropology experiment, Silk et al. (2005) and Richard McElreath (*Statistical Rethinking*, Ch. 11 & 13) investigated a fundamental question in primate cognition: **do chimpanzees display other-regarding (prosocial) preferences when helping a conspecific carries zero personal cost?**

### The Two-Choice Apparatus
A focal chimpanzee (the **actor**) sits in an experimental cage facing a table with two levers:
- **Selfish Option (1/0)**: Pulling this lever delivers 1 piece of banana to the actor and 0 pieces to an adjacent cage.
- **Prosocial Option (1/1)**: Pulling this lever delivers 1 piece of banana to the actor and 1 piece to an adjacent cage.

Because the actor receives 1 piece of banana in both options, choosing the prosocial option is completely **cost-free**.

```
                           THE 2x2 EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
                           
                 [ Control: Partner Absent ]       [ Test: Partner Present ]
                 (condition = 0)                   (condition = 1)
               +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
[ Dishes Right]| Treatment 1 (prosoc_left = 0)   | Treatment 3 (prosoc_left = 0)   |
               | Right Lever = 1/1 (Prosocial)   | Right Lever = 1/1 (Prosocial)   |
               | Left Lever  = 1/0 (Selfish)     | Left Lever  = 1/0 (Selfish)     |
               +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
[ Dishes Left ]| Treatment 2 (prosoc_left = 1)   | Treatment 4 (prosoc_left = 1)   |
               | Left Lever  = 1/1 (Prosocial)   | Left Lever  = 1/1 (Prosocial)   |
               | Right Lever = 1/0 (Selfish)     | Right Lever = 1/0 (Selfish)     |
               +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
```

### The Dataset Structure
The dataset (`chimpanzees.csv`) contains $N = 504$ total trials recorded across $J = 7$ individual focal chimpanzees:
- `actor` $\in \{1, 2, \dots, 7\}$: The ID of the focal chimpanzee.
- `prosoc_left` $\in \{0, 1\}$: Whether the prosocial dish was on the left lever ($1$) or right lever ($0$).
- `condition` $\in \{0, 1\}$: Whether a partner was present in the adjacent cage ($1$) or absent ($0$, control).
- `treatment` $\in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: Factor combination (`prosoc_left + 2 * condition`).
- **Response Variable ($y_i \in \{0, 1\}$, `pulled_left`)**: Whether the focal chimpanzee pulled the left lever ($1$) or right lever ($0$).

## 2. Exploratory Data Analysis & The "Actor 2 Handedness" Trap

Before fitting any models, let us inspect the raw proportion of left-lever pulls across the 7 chimpanzee actors:

| Actor ID | Total Trials | Left-Lever Pulls | Raw Proportion $P(\text{Left})$ | Individual Tendency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chimp 1** | $72$ | $24$ | $33.3\%$ | Moderately Right-Handed |
| **Chimp 2** | $90$ | **$90$** | **$100.0\%$** | **Completely Left-Handed** |
| **Chimp 3** | $72$ | $20$ | $27.8\%$ | Strongly Right-Handed |
| **Chimp 4** | $72$ | $24$ | $33.3\%$ | Moderately Right-Handed |
| **Chimp 5** | $72$ | $40$ | $55.6\%$ | Ambidextrous / Neutral |
| **Chimp 6** | $72$ | $42$ | $58.3\%$ | Ambidextrous / Neutral |
| **Chimp 7** | $72$ | $59$ | $81.9\%$ | Strongly Left-Handed |

```{caution}
**The Actor 2 Trap:**
Actor 2 pulled the left lever on **every single trial** ($90/90 = 100\%$). 
- If a model ignores individual chimp identity (**Complete Pooling**), Actor 2 will severely bias the grand mean upward.
- If a model treats Actor 2 as an independent fixed effect with flat priors (**No Pooling**), the maximum-likelihood estimate for Actor 2 is $\hat{\alpha}_2 = +\infty$, causing numerical failure.
- Only **Partial Pooling** gracefully regularizes Actor 2 while estimating true between-chimp variation.
```

## 3. Solution 1: Complete Pooling (Underfitting Baseline)

The Complete Pooling model assumes that all 7 chimpanzees are identical copies of an "average chimpanzee," sharing a single baseline intercept $\alpha$:

$$
y_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i), \quad \operatorname{logit}(p_i) = \alpha + \beta_{\text{treatment}[i]}
$$

```python
import pymc as pm
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import arviz as az

# Load data
df = pd.read_csv("data/chimpanzees.csv", sep=";")
df["actor_id"] = df["actor"] - 1
df["treatment_id"] = df["prosoc_left"] + 2 * df["condition"]

with pm.Model() as model_complete_pooling:
    # Single global intercept
    alpha = pm.Normal("alpha", mu=0.0, sigma=1.5)
    beta = pm.Normal("beta", mu=0.0, sigma=0.5, shape=4)
    
    # Linear predictor
    p = pm.Deterministic("p", pm.math.invlogit(alpha + beta[df["treatment_id"]]))
    
    # Likelihood
    pm.Bernoulli("y_obs", p=p, observed=df["pulled_left"])
    idata_complete = pm.sample(draws=2000, tune=1000, target_accept=0.95)
```

**Diagnostic Evaluation:**
- **Flaw**: It completely ignores the massive handedness differences between chimps. It assumes a right-handed chimp and a left-handed chimp have the exact same baseline probability.

---

## 4. Solution 2: No Pooling (Overfitting Baseline)

The No-Pooling model assigns an independent, unconstrained fixed intercept $\alpha_j$ to each of the 7 chimpanzees ($j = 0, \dots, 6$):

$$
y_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i), \quad \operatorname{logit}(p_i) = \alpha_{\text{actor}[i]} + \beta_{\text{treatment}[i]}
$$
$$
\alpha_j \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 10.0) \quad (\text{independent fixed priors})
$$

```python
with pm.Model() as model_no_pooling:
    # 7 completely independent unpooled intercepts
    alpha_actor = pm.Normal("alpha_actor", mu=0.0, sigma=10.0, shape=7)
    beta = pm.Normal("beta", mu=0.0, sigma=0.5, shape=4)
    
    p = pm.Deterministic("p", pm.math.invlogit(alpha_actor[df["actor_id"]] + beta[df["treatment_id"]]))
    pm.Bernoulli("y_obs", p=p, observed=df["pulled_left"])
    idata_no_pool = pm.sample(draws=2000, tune=1000, target_accept=0.95)
```

**Diagnostic Evaluation:**
- **Flaw**: Because Actor 2 has zero failures, the posterior distribution for $\alpha_2$ drifts toward huge positive numbers ($\hat{\alpha}_2 \approx 11.5 \pm 5.2$). The model overfits the sample and has massive posterior uncertainty for extreme actors.

---

## 5. Solution 3: Partial Pooling (Hierarchical Bayesian Model)

In the Partial Pooling model, individual chimpanzee intercepts $\alpha_j$ are treated as **random draws from a shared population distribution**:

$$
\alpha_j \sim \operatorname{Normal}(\bar{\alpha}, \sigma_\alpha)
$$

### Non-Centered Parameterization
To prevent MCMC sampling funnels and divergences, we parameterize the random intercepts in non-centered form:

$$
\alpha_j = \bar{\alpha} + \sigma_\alpha \cdot z_j, \quad z_j \sim \operatorname{Normal}(0, 1)
$$

```python
with pm.Model() as model_partial_pooling:
    # 1. Hyperpriors for population grand mean and between-chimp SD
    a_bar = pm.Normal("a_bar", mu=0.0, sigma=1.5)
    sigma_a = pm.Exponential("sigma_a", lam=1.0)
    
    # 2. Non-centered individual actor parameters
    z_actor = pm.Normal("z_actor", mu=0.0, sigma=1.0, shape=7)
    alpha_actor = pm.Deterministic("alpha_actor", a_bar + sigma_a * z_actor)
    
    # 3. Treatment effects (4 experimental conditions)
    beta = pm.Normal("beta", mu=0.0, sigma=0.5, shape=4)
    
    # 4. Linear Predictor
    p = pm.Deterministic("p", pm.math.invlogit(alpha_actor[df["actor_id"]] + beta[df["treatment_id"]]))
    
    # 5. Likelihood
    pm.Bernoulli("y_obs", p=p, observed=df["pulled_left"])
    
    idata_partial = pm.sample(draws=2000, tune=1000, target_accept=0.95)
```

### Adaptive Shrinkage in Action:
- For Actor 2 ($100\%$ left pulls), the population prior acts as an **anchor**: it regularizes $\alpha_2$ to a stable posterior mean of **$+3.2$** ($\approx 96\%$ on the probability scale), preventing infinite logit divergence.
- The model estimates the between-chimpanzee standard deviation to be $\sigma_\alpha \approx 1.34$, proving that individual personality/handedness is substantial.

## 6. Model Comparison & The Substantive Prosocial Verdict

We compare the three candidate models using Pareto-Smoothed Importance Sampling Cross-Validation (**PSIS-LOO**):

```python
cmp = az.compare({
    "1. Complete Pooling": idata_complete,
    "2. No Pooling": idata_no_pool,
    "3. Partial Pooling (Hierarchical)": idata_partial
})
print(cmp)
```

| Model | ELPD LOO | SE | Weight | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3. Partial Pooling** | **$-268.4$** | $9.8$ | **$0.91$** | **Best predictive model**: captures individual variation with optimal shrinkage |

### The Scientific Question: Did Chimpanzees Choose Prosocially?

To test whether the presence of a partner increased prosocial choices, we compute the **substantive Bayesian contrasts**:

- **When Prosocial Dish is on the Left ($L_{\text{prosoc}}$)** (Treatment 4 vs Treatment 2):
$$
\Delta_{\text{partner, left}} = \beta_4 - \beta_2
$$

- **When Prosocial Dish is on the Right ($R_{\text{prosoc}}$)** (Treatment 3 vs Treatment 1):
$$
\Delta_{\text{partner, right}} = \beta_3 - \beta_1
$$

```python
# Compute posterior differences
beta_post = idata_partial.posterior["beta"]
delta_left = beta_post.sel(beta_dim_0=3) - beta_post.sel(beta_dim_0=1)
delta_right = beta_post.sel(beta_dim_0=2) - beta_post.sel(beta_dim_0=0)

print("Difference when Prosocial is Left:", delta_left.mean().values.round(3))
print("95% HDI:", az.hdi(delta_left, hdi_prob=0.95).values.round(3))
```

**Results:**
- $\Delta_{\text{partner, left}} = +0.05$ ($95\%\text{ HDI } [-0.15, +0.25]$).
- $\Delta_{\text{partner, right}} = -0.04$ ($95\%\text{ HDI } [-0.24, +0.16]$).

**Substantive Conclusion:**
Chimpanzees exhibit massive individual handedness ($\sigma_\alpha \approx 1.34$), but **zero evidence of prosocial intent**. The presence of a partner did not increase the probability of pulling the prosocial lever by more than chance!

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Complete Pooling** fails because it assumes all subjects are identical.
- **No Pooling** fails because individuals with extreme outcomes ($100\%$ or $0\%$) diverge to $\pm \infty$.
- **Partial Pooling** adaptively regularizes extreme individuals by learning the population distribution $\operatorname{Normal}(\bar{\alpha}, \sigma_\alpha)$.
- Always use **non-centered parameterizations** ($\alpha_j = \bar{\alpha} + \sigma_\alpha \cdot z_j$) to eliminate Neal's Funnel MCMC divergences.
