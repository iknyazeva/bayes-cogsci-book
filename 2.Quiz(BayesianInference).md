## Political Preferences

**For Questions 1–5, consider the following scenario:**

You are trying to ascertain your American colleague's political preferences. To do so, you design a questionnaire with five yes/no questions relating to current issues. The questions are all worded so that a "yes" response indicates a conservative viewpoint.

Let $\theta$ be the unknown political viewpoint of your colleague, which we will assume can only take values $\theta = \text{conservative}$ or $\theta = \text{liberal}$. You have no reason to believe that your colleague leans one way or the other, so you assign the prior $P(\theta = \text{conservative}) = 0.5$.

**Question 1.** Assume the five questions are independent and let $Y$ count the number of "yes" responses. If your colleague is conservative, then the probability of a "yes" response on any given question is 0.8. If your colleague is liberal, the probability of a "no" response on any given question is 0.7. Which of the following is the correct likelihood?

1. $f(y \mid \theta) = \frac{\theta^y e^{-\theta}}{y!}$
2. $f(y \mid \theta) = \binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}$
3. $f(y \mid \theta) = \binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y} I_{\theta = \text{conservative}} + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y} I_{\theta = \text{liberal}}$
4. $f(y \mid \theta) = \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y} I_{\theta = \text{conservative}} + \binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y} I_{\theta = \text{liberal}}$

**Question 2.** Suppose you ask your colleague the five questions and he answers "no" to all of them. What is the MLE for $\theta$?

1. $\hat{\theta} = \text{conservative}$
2. $\hat{\theta} = \text{liberal}$
3. None of the above. The MLE is a number.

**Question 3.** What is the corresponding expression for the posterior probability?

1. $f(\theta \mid y) = \frac{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.2) I_{\theta=\text{conservative}} + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y}(0.7) I_{\theta=\text{liberal}}}{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.2) + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y}(0.7)}$
2. $f(\theta \mid y) = \frac{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.5) I_{\theta=\text{conservative}} + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y}(0.5) I_{\theta=\text{liberal}}}{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.5) + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y}(0.5)}$
3. $f(\theta \mid y) = \frac{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.2)(0.5)^2}{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.5) + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y}(0.5)}$
4. $f(\theta \mid y) = \frac{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.5) + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y}(0.5)}{\binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y}(0.5) + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y}(0.5)}$

**Question 4.** Evaluate the expression in Question 3 for $y = 0$ and report the posterior probability that your colleague is conservative, given that he responded "no" to all of the questions. Round your answer to three decimal places.

**Question 5.** Evaluate the expression in Question 3 for $y = 0$ and report the posterior probability that your colleague is liberal, given that he responded "no" to all of the questions. Round your answer to three decimal places.


## Loaded Coins

**Question 6.** Recall that your brother has a fair coin which comes up heads 50% of the time and a loaded coin which comes up heads 70% of the time. Suppose now that he has a third coin which comes up tails 70% of the time. Again, you don't know which coin your brother has brought you, so you are going to test it by flipping it 4 times, where $X$ counts the number of heads.

Let $\theta$ identify the coin so that there are three possibilities: $\theta = \text{fair}$, $\theta = \text{loaded\_heads}$, and $\theta = \text{loaded\_tails}$.

Suppose the prior is: $P(\theta = \text{fair}) = 0.4$, $P(\theta = \text{loaded\_heads}) = 0.3$, $P(\theta = \text{loaded\_tails}) = 0.3$.

Our prior probability that the coin is loaded is still 0.6, but we do not know which loaded coin it is, so we split the probability evenly between the two options. What is the form of the likelihood?

1. $f(x \mid \theta) = \binom{4}{x} 0.5^4 I_{\theta=\text{fair}} + \binom{4}{x} 0.7^x 0.3^{4-x} I_{\theta=\text{loaded\_heads}} + \binom{4}{x} 0.3^x 0.7^{4-x} I_{\theta=\text{loaded\_tails}}$
2. $f(x \mid \theta) = \binom{4}{x} 0.5^4 I_{\theta=\text{fair}} + \binom{4}{x} 0.3^x 0.7^{4-x} I_{\theta=\text{loaded\_heads}} + \binom{4}{x} 0.7^x 0.3^{4-x} I_{\theta=\text{loaded\_tails}}$
3. $f(x \mid \theta) = \binom{4}{x} 0.5^4 (0.4) I_{\theta=\text{fair}} + \binom{4}{x} 0.3^x 0.7^{4-x} (0.3) I_{\theta=\text{loaded\_heads}} + \binom{4}{x} 0.7^x 0.3^{4-x} (0.3) I_{\theta=\text{loaded\_tails}}$

**Question 7.** Suppose you flip the coin four times and it comes up heads twice. What is the MLE for $\theta$?

1. $\hat{\theta} = \text{fair}$
2. $\hat{\theta} = \text{loaded\_heads}$
3. $\hat{\theta} = \text{loaded\_tails}$
4. None of the above. The MLE is a number.

**Question 8.** Suppose you flip the coin four times and it comes up heads twice. What is the posterior probability that this is the fair coin? Round your answer to two decimal places.

**Question 9.** Suppose you flip the coin four times and it comes up heads twice. What is the posterior probability that this is a loaded coin (favoring either heads or tails)? Round your answer to two decimal places.


## Signal Detection in a Psychophysics Experiment

A participant performs a visual detection task. On each trial, a faint stimulus is presented and the participant reports whether they saw it (yes/no). You model their detection rate $\theta$ with a uniform prior $\text{Beta}(1, 1)$ on $[0, 1]$.

In 20 trials, the participant detects the stimulus 14 times.

**Question 10.** Write the likelihood function for this data, treating each trial as an independent Bernoulli observation with success probability $\theta$.

**Question 11.** What is the MLE for $\theta$?

**Question 12.** What is the posterior distribution for $\theta$? (Hint: use the Beta-Binomial conjugacy rule: $\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha, \beta) + k\text{ successes in } N\text{ trials} \to \operatorname{Beta}(\alpha+k, \beta+N-k)$.)

**Question 13.** Compute the posterior mean and compare it to the MLE. Why are they different? In which direction does the prior "pull" the estimate, and why?


## Answers and Solutions

:::{dropdown} Click to view Solutions for Political Preferences (Questions 1–5)
- **Q1**: **Option 3** — The likelihood conditions on $\theta$, selecting the correct success probability ($0.8$ if conservative, $1 - 0.7 = 0.3$ if liberal) via indicators:
  $$f(y \mid \theta) = \binom{5}{y} 0.8^y 0.2^{5-y} I_{\theta = \text{conservative}} + \binom{5}{y} 0.3^y 0.7^{5-y} I_{\theta = \text{liberal}}$$
- **Q2**: **Option 2 ($\hat{\theta} = \text{liberal}$)** — For $y = 0$:
  - $L(\text{conservative} \mid y=0) = 0.2^5 = 0.00032$.
  - $L(\text{liberal} \mid y=0) = 0.7^5 = 0.16807$.
  Since $0.16807 > 0.00032$, the likelihood is maximized at $\theta = \text{liberal}$. (Parameters can be discrete labels as well as numbers).
- **Q3**: **Option 2** — By Bayes' rule, the posterior is:
  $$P(\theta \mid y) = \frac{f(y \mid \theta)P(\theta)}{\sum_{\theta'} f(y \mid \theta')P(\theta')}$$
  Since $P(\text{conservative}) = P(\text{liberal}) = 0.5$, Option 2 correctly specifies the posterior mass function.
- **Q4**: **0.002** —
  $$P(\text{conservative} \mid y=0) = \frac{0.00032 \times 0.5}{(0.00032 \times 0.5) + (0.16807 \times 0.5)} = \frac{0.00016}{0.00016 + 0.084035} \approx 0.0019 \approx 0.002$$
- **Q5**: **0.998** —
  $$P(\text{liberal} \mid y=0) = 1 - 0.0019 = 0.9981 \approx 0.998$$
:::

:::{dropdown} Click to view Solutions for Loaded Coins (Questions 6–9)
- **Q6**: **Option 1** — The likelihood function specifies $P(x \mid \theta)$ for each hypothesis without multiplying by prior probabilities.
- **Q7**: **Option 1 ($\hat{\theta} = \text{fair}$)** — For $x = 2$ heads in 4 flips:
  - Fair: $\binom{4}{2}(0.5)^4 = 6 \times 0.0625 = 0.375$.
  - Loaded Heads: $\binom{4}{2}(0.7)^2(0.3)^2 = 6 \times 0.49 \times 0.09 = 0.2646$.
  - Loaded Tails: $\binom{4}{2}(0.3)^2(0.7)^2 = 6 \times 0.09 \times 0.49 = 0.2646$.
  Since $0.375 > 0.2646$, the MLE is $\hat{\theta} = \text{fair}$.
- **Q8**: **0.49** — Multiplying prior $\times$ likelihood:
  - Fair: $0.4 \times 0.375 = 0.150$
  - Loaded Heads: $0.3 \times 0.2646 = 0.07938$
  - Loaded Tails: $0.3 \times 0.2646 = 0.07938$
  - Marginal $P(x=2) = 0.150 + 0.07938 + 0.07938 = 0.30876$.
  $$P(\text{fair} \mid x=2) = \frac{0.150}{0.30876} \approx 0.4858 \approx 0.49$$
- **Q9**: **0.51** —
  $$P(\text{loaded} \mid x=2) = 1 - P(\text{fair} \mid x=2) = 1 - 0.4858 = 0.5142 \approx 0.51$$
  Notice that even though the fair coin has the highest individual likelihood, the loaded coins collectively hold $51\%$ of the posterior probability because their combined prior was $0.60$!
:::

:::{dropdown} Click to view Solutions for Signal Detection (Questions 10–13)
- **Q10**: $L(\theta \mid y) = \binom{20}{14} \theta^{14} (1 - \theta)^6$, or proportional to $\theta^{14} (1 - \theta)^6$.
- **Q11**: $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} = \frac{k}{N} = \frac{14}{20} = 0.70$.
- **Q12**: Prior $\operatorname{Beta}(1, 1)$ with $k=14$ and $N-k=6$ updates to:
  $$\theta \mid y \sim \operatorname{Beta}(1 + 14, \; 1 + 6) = \operatorname{Beta}(15, 7)$$
- **Q13**:
  - Posterior mean $= \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta} = \frac{15}{15 + 7} = \frac{15}{22} \approx 0.682$.
  - Comparison: The posterior mean ($0.682$) is slightly lower than the sample proportion MLE ($0.70$).
  - Explanation: The uniform prior $\operatorname{Beta}(1, 1)$ acts like two virtual observations (one success and one failure) with a prior mean of $0.50$. This prior information exerts a slight shrinkage pull toward $0.50$, regularizing the estimate.
:::
