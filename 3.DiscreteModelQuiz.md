## Question 22.  Poisson for eye blink rate

A researcher notices that participants with high cognitive load tend to blink more frequently during a sustained attention task. She decides a Poisson process model is appropriate for counting blinks, with blink rate $\theta$ blinks per participant per minute.

The model for the total number of blinks is then

$$
Y \sim \text{Poisson}(n \cdot t \cdot \theta)
$$

where $n$ is the number of participants in the group and $t$ is the number of minutes observed. That is, if we observe blinks from a group of 24 participants for 5 minutes, the expected number of blinks would be $24 \cdot 5 \cdot \theta = 120 \cdot \theta.$
The likelihood for $Y$ is then

$$
f(y \mid \theta) = \frac{(n t \theta)^y e^{-n t \theta}}{y!} \propto \theta^y e^{-n t \theta}.
$$

This model also has a conjugate gamma prior $\theta \sim \text{Gamma}(a, b)$
which has density:

$$
f(\theta) = \frac{b^a}{\Gamma(a)} \theta^{a-1} e^{-b \theta} \propto \theta^{a-1} e^{-b \theta}.
$$

Find the posterior distribution for $\theta$.

The baseline spontaneous blink rate is approximately 15 blinks per minute. To be conservative, the researcher sets the prior mean for $\theta$ at 15 with a large standard deviation of 10. Suppose there are $n = 24$ participants in the high-load group, and the researcher monitors blinks for $t = 5$ minutes. She observes a total of $y = 2160$ blinks from the group. Draw a vertical dashed line at the baseline rate of 15 blinks/min, and plot the prior and posterior distributions. Does this posterior inference for $\theta$ suggest that the high-load group has a higher blink rate than the baseline of 15 blinks per minute?
